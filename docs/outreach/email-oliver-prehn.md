**Subject:** Your "Scales of Harmonies" in a small open-source app — asking for your permission

Dear Mr. Prehn,

I am writing to ask your permission for something, and to thank you first.

Your lesson "All About Musical Scales" and its overview sheet, "The Scales of Harmonies",
changed the way I hear and play. Seeing thirty-three scales fall out of four simple rules,
ordered into seven families, each family a single pattern read from a different degree —
that is the clearest map of harmony I have ever been given. [STEVE: one or two sentences
of your own here — how you came across the lesson, what it changed in your playing. It
must be true and in your words; this is the part he will actually read.] Your teaching has
the rare quality of making deep things feel evident, and I am one of many musicians in
your debt.

I am a hobbyist musician and developer. I have built a small Android app, DuoMix Fader,
which lets me play along with the music I am listening to. One of its features estimates,
while a track plays, which of your seven families and thirty-three scales the music is
using, its tonic, and its chord progression. It is meant as an accompaniment aid, nothing
more. The detection method is my own work; but the *map* it points to — the families, the
thirty-three scales, your systematic names, the chords — is entirely yours, and the app
says so.

Nothing has been published. The app currently runs on my own phone only. Before I share
it with anyone, I want to ask you properly, because I could find no licence for your
material on your site and I will not assume one.

Concretely, this is what the app takes from your work:

1. It classifies music using your seven families and thirty-three scales, and displays,
   for each detected scale, the five columns of your overview table: family, popular name,
   interval steps, systematic name, chords.
2. It bundles a one-page study sheet, attached, that reproduces the data of your table.
   I re-typeset it and added a colour code: blue and red are the two whole-tone sets of
   the keyboard, so a whole step stays in its colour and a half step or a step and a half
   crosses to the other; the interval steps are drawn on a semitone grid, which makes your
   staircase alignment of the modes visible at a glance. Your introduction and footnote
   are paraphrased rather than copied. My own handwritten notes are left out.
3. Your name, the lesson, www.newjazz.dk and your Patreon and PayPal links appear at the
   top of that sheet, in the app's Harmony screen under "Credits" with a "Support on
   Patreon" button, and in the project's notices. The app states that it is not affiliated
   with or endorsed by you.

The app is free and open source (Apache 2.0 for my code; your material is explicitly
excluded from that licence and marked as yours). It contains no advertising, has no
network access, and nothing it analyses ever leaves the phone. I have attached a short user guide with screenshots so you can see exactly how
your work appears.

I would be grateful to know which of these you would prefer:

- you are happy for the app to be shared as it is;
- you would allow it with changes — different wording of the credit, other links, leaving
  out the study sheet or the systematic names, or anything else you wish;
- you would rather your material were not included. In that case I will remove it before
  anything is published, without any hard feelings, and keep the app for my own practice.

Whatever you decide, I will follow it, and if you ever change your mind later I will
follow that too. If it is useful, I would gladly send you the source of the colour-coded
sheet to use or adapt however you like — it is the least I can offer in return.

Thank you for your time, and above all for the music and the teaching.

With great respect and gratitude,

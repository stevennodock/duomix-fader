1 · What it does

Android normally lets one app play at a time: start a video and your music pauses. DuoMix Fader removes that limit for two apps you choose — a music app and a video or voice app — and gives you a two-channel mixer with a DJ-style, constant-power crossfader. It needs no root.

On top of the mixer, the Harmony feature listens to the music channel and estimates the scale of the piece among the 7 families and 33 Scales of Harmonies described by Oliver Prehn (NewJazz), its tonic, and its chord progression in Roman numerals. It is an accompaniment aid: a steady, averaged reading of the piece, not a note-by-note transcription.

DuoMix Fader is an independent project. It is not affiliated with Google, YouTube, the Shizuku project or Oliver Prehn.

2 · Getting started

DuoMix Fader relies on Shizuku, a separate free app that lets it perform a few system-level audio operations without root.

  1. Install Shizuku and start it through Developer options → Wireless debugging, following Shizuku's own instructions. After a reboot it has to be started again, unless you enabled its start-on-boot option.
  2. Open DuoMix Fader and tap Request Shizuku permission, then accept.
  3. When the three lights are green, you are ready.
If the first light is red, Shizuku is not running: open Shizuku and start it. Nothing in DuoMix Fader works without it. On a OnePlus, OPPO or realme phone, or on Android 12, read section 10 first.

Languages

The app follows your phone's language — English, French or Spanish. You can also choose its language alone in Settings → System → Languages → App languages. Scale names are international musical terms and are never translated.

3 · The mixer

Apps to mix

Pick one app per channel. Only supported apps that are installed are listed: for music, YouTube Music, Spotify, Deezer, Apple Music, Amazon Music, SoundCloud, Tidal, Qobuz — and YouTube, X and Telegram, since music is also played from videos and feeds (Harmony listens to the music channel); for video and voice, YouTube, X, Telegram, Twitch, VLC, Netflix, Pocket Casts, Chrome, Firefox. An app can hold only one channel: pick it on a channel while the other one has it, and the two channels swap. The list is closed on purpose: the privileged service refuses any other app.

Simultaneous playback

Android pauses an app when another one takes the audio focus. Turn the switch on for an app and it stops being paused by the other. Enabling it for the music app is usually enough.

Some apps pause themselves when they are refused audio focus (X does). Their switch is disabled and says so: enable the option on the other channel instead.

Volumes and crossfader

Each slider sets the volume of one app only. The crossfader moves from the music app (left) to the video app (right) following an equal-power curve: at the centre both sit at 71 %, so the overall loudness stays constant.

When you close both the screen and the notification, both apps get their full volume back, and switching a channel to another app restores the app you leave.

4 · The fader in your notifications

You do not have to keep the app open. While DuoMix Fader runs, a media-player card called DuoMix fader appears next to your other players — in the notification shade and on the lock screen.

DuoMix Fader plays no sound itself: this player card is only a control surface, which is why it has no play button. Its layout, its two text lines and their size belong to Android. Nothing scrolls or blinks on the card: it only changes when the scale, the piece or the balance does.

The history widget

Long-press an empty spot of your home screen, choose Widgets, then DuoMix · Detection history. It shows the pieces you listened to — the one playing first — each with its key, its scale and colour tiles, the scales held in turn with their degree, and the chord progression. Scroll the list; tap it to open the history in the app. It is updated only when its content changes, never on a timer, and it is not offered on the lock screen: the player card is there for that.

5 · Harmony: scale, tonic, chords

Open it from the ♪ Harmony card of the main screen (Scale details) or from the ♪ button of the notification. Analysis starts by itself as soon as the music app plays.

What you read

  • The scale — tonic and popular name, then its family, systematic name and chords, as in Oliver Prehn's table. The tonic is written in your language's system, with the other one next to it: “B♭ (Si♭)”.
  • Confidence — how clearly this set of notes beats the next candidate. Below about 15 %, treat the family as a hint.
  • Scales detected in this piece — one row per scale held, with the time it appeared and the five columns of the table. Scroll the table sideways.
  • Chord progression — Roman numerals relative to the tonic, the chord names beneath, and the length of the loop.

Reading the numerals

Degrees are read on the major scale of the tonic, with ♭ or ♯ for other pitches: in A minor, F major is written ♭VI. Upper case is major (+ for augmented), lower case is minor (° for diminished).

Analyse again

The ⟳ button forgets everything and analyses the current piece afresh — useful if you started listening in the middle of an unusual passage.

The reading is deliberately slow and steady. The first estimate needs about 8 seconds of music; a change of scale is only accepted if it lasts 12 seconds, a change of mode 30 seconds. Expect the first minute of a piece to be the least reliable.

6 · What Harmony listens to: app sound or microphone

Under the scale, the line Source offers two buttons. You can switch at any time; the analysis, the history, the widget and the notification card follow whichever source is active.

SourceWhat it hears
App soundThe sound of the music app only, taken inside the phone: it works with headphones, and the other channel never enters the analysis. On recent versions of Android the privileged service captures it. On Android 12 it cannot, so DuoMix Fader uses Android's own playback capture instead: the first time, Android asks for the recording permission — it is used only to read the music app's sound, the screen is never captured — and may show a cast icon in the status bar while it runs.
🎙 MicrophoneWhat the phone hears: its own speaker, a stereo in the room, an instrument you play. It hears nothing of a headphone session. Tap it once to start (Android asks for the microphone permission the first time), tap it again to stop. It is never switched on by itself.

Microphone settings

  • Gain, 0 to +40 dB. A phone microphone is quiet when it is read without processing, and music below a certain level is treated as silence. Raise the gain until the scope comes alive.
  • Bass and Treble, −12 to +12 dB. Lifting the bass helps the bass line and the tonic; lowering the treble sets room noise aside. At 0 dB the sound is left untouched.
  • The scope shows three lanes — treble, mids, bass — taken after these settings, at a fixed scale. Flat traces: raise the gain. Traces shaved off at the top and bottom: it clips, lower it. Waves filling about half of each lane on loud passages are about right.
When the microphone is off and the music app is switched off under Simultaneous playback, nothing is listened to: the block says Not listening. Turn one of the two back on. An app whose switch cannot be turned on (X) is still analysed.

7 · History, one section per piece

Tap the Chord progression card of the Harmony panel to open the history. The piece being played comes first, marked now playing; earlier pieces follow, most recent first, up to fifty.

Each section reads in three levels, separated by thin lines:

  • The main scale of the piece, under its name: the key in large letters, the scale and its colour tiles.
  • The scales held in turn, one row each, in fixed columns: the time, the degree in Roman numerals relative to the main key, the tonic, the tiles. Read down a column to follow the move from one degree to the next.
  • The chord progression, at the foot: one cell per chord, the degree above its name, and the length of the loop.

Pieces are told apart by the title and artist that the music app announces itself — the same information your lock screen shows. Nothing is recognised by listening and nothing is looked up online. When an app announces no title, a silence of a few seconds marks the boundary and the section is called Unknown piece.

Clear empties the list. The history is a private file of the app; it contains names of pieces, scales and chords, never any sound.

8 · The Scales of Harmonies sheet

The link 📄 The 33 scales, colour-coded (PDF), in the Harmony card and in the panel, opens a one-page study sheet inside the app. Pinch to zoom, drag to move around. It exists in English, French and Spanish and follows the app's language.

The sheet reproduces the data of Oliver Prehn's overview — 33 scales in 7 families, with popular name, interval steps, systematic name and chords — and adds a colour code:

  • Blue and red are the two whole-tone sets of the keyboard: C D E F♯ G♯ A♯ and C♯ D♯ F G A B.
  • Each scale is a row of square tiles, one per note, from the tonic to its octave — the same notation as the notification card and the history. A whole step stays in the same colour; a half step or a step and a half crosses to the other.
  • A tile split in two is reached by a step and a half: the colour you come from on top, the one you arrive at below. A plain change of colour is a half step. Only families 3, 4 and 7 contain steps and a half. The same mark is used in the app, the widget and the notification.
  • Colours are given with the first scale of each family starting on C. The scales of a family line up as a staircase — each one is the family's pattern read from the next degree — so every column is one and the same note. From a red tonic (C♯, F, G, A…), swap the two colours: the app always shows the real ones.
  • Each family has its own colour; the scales printed in bold in the original are bold here too.
The musical content of this sheet is Oliver Prehn's work and remains his. Only the layout and the colour code were added. See Credits.

9 · How the analysis works, and its limits

  1. Notes → family. The energy of the 12 pitch classes is averaged over about thirty seconds. The set of notes that explains it best gives the family. When two candidates are close, the more common family wins.
  2. Chords. Short frames are matched against the 48 triads. Pitch classes ignore octaves, so a chord is recognised whatever its inversion or voicing.
  3. Loop. The music is compared with itself to find the period at which the chords come back. All repetitions are stacked and averaged: what returns every time — the chords — is reinforced; melody, voice and drums fade. If no loop is found, the last 20 seconds are read as they are, and the panel says so.
  4. Tonic → mode. The modes of a family share the same notes, so notes alone cannot tell D Dorian from C major. The tonic is the note where the piece spends the most time on the chord that this mode builds on its first degree — the “Chords” column of the table — backed by the bass line.

Limits you should know

  • The mode is less certain than the family, and sometimes the ambiguity is musical: a loop such as I – V – vi – IV spends as much time on four chords, each of which is the tonic chord of a mode of the same family.
  • Rootless jazz voicings can hide a chord (a rootless Cmaj9 is an Em7); the bass line is then the only clue.
  • Chords shorter than about half a second, music tuned far from A = 440 Hz, and very percussive mixes are read poorly. Four-note chords are reduced to their triad.
  • If the music channel is faded to zero, there is nothing left to analyse.
  • The method has been tested on synthetic signals and on a handful of real pieces, not on an annotated corpus: its success rate on real music is unknown.

The full method, its constants and what did not work are described in the research note shipped with the source code, docs/research/harmony-analysis.md.

10 · Phones and Android versions

DuoMix Fader acts through the rights that Android gives to its shell identity, and those rights are not the same on every version or brand. The app checks what your phone allows when it starts, switches off what it cannot do, and says so on the screen. It installs from Android 12.

Switch mode

Android 12 does not let the shell reach the players of other apps, so their volume cannot be set. The shell can still switch an app's sound off and on. The fader then becomes a three-way switch — music alone on the left, both in the middle, video alone on the right — and each channel slider mutes its app below 40 %. The mixer card says Switch mode and shows each channel as on or muted. Whatever was muted gets its sound back when DuoMix Fader stops, and again the next time it starts, should it ever be killed in between.

The notification on Android 12

The media-player card of Android 12 cannot show a scale: its text line lets only two tiles through, and it shrinks the artwork to a small square. There, DuoMix Fader posts an ordinary notification instead, outside the media area. Collapsed, it shows the key and the eight tiles in a row. Expanded, it adds the name of the mode, the chord progression, the piece, and the fader as three buttons — music app, Both, video app, the current one outlined — next to ♪ and ✕.

OnePlus, OPPO and realme

These brands remove some rights from adb — and therefore from Shizuku — until you turn on Disable permission monitoring in Developer options, then restart Shizuku. Without it Shizuku warns that “the permission of adb is limited” and DuoMix Fader cannot work; the app reminds you of the option. It lifts a protection that these brands add on top of Android for a computer connected in debugging mode, and gives adb back the behaviour it has on other phones: decide for yourself whether that suits the phone you use.

Versions between Android 13 and 16, and other brands, have not been tested. The app does not guess from the version number: it reads what the shell is allowed to do on the phone in hand.

11 · Privacy

  • The app has no network permission. Nothing it hears, reads or stores can leave the phone.
  • To analyse a piece it listens to the music app only — or, if you choose it, to the phone's microphone. Either way the sound is reduced on the spot to twelve numbers per instant (and, for the microphone, to the picture of the scope) and discarded; it is never recorded, and the microphone is never switched on by itself.
  • The recording permission is asked only when you choose the microphone, or App sound on Android 12, where Android's playback capture requires it. There, the capture is limited to the music app's sound; the screen is never captured.
  • It reads the title and artist that the music app publishes for the lock screen, to name the sections of the history.
  • The history is a private file; Clear deletes it. Uninstalling the app removes everything.
  • The privileged service it starts through Shizuku can only do a short, fixed list of things — change the audio-focus setting and the volume of the listed apps (or, on Android 12, switch their sound off and on), listen to the music app, read its title, open DuoMix Fader's own screen, and allow DuoMix Fader itself to use Android's playback capture. It runs no arbitrary command and stops by itself when the app stops.
